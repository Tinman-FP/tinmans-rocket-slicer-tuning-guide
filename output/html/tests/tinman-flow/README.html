Tinman flow tiles for Rocket

Use the original Tinman and Orca Flow Pass 1 and Pass 2 geometry, sliced by Rocket. This pack contains 19 independent 40 × 30 × 2 mm meshes, their original editable sources, conversion script and separate AGPL notices. There is no G-code, machine target, material preset or automatic flow schedule.

Do not import all tiles and assume their labels change flow. In this recipe, slice one tile per job with one explicitly entered material Flow multiplier. Rocket does not execute Tinman's calibration generator when it imports an STL.

Before using this test

Establish temperature and a usable throughput region first. Keep any unsupported PA stage marked unperformed; preserve supported OEM pressure behavior. A nozzle leak, feed problem, damp filament or incorrect first-layer gap invalidates the comparison. This first pack addresses ordinary FFF, not CFC/matrix qualification.

Use the actual machine, ordinary FFF nozzle, spool and plate. Start with a copied, appropriately named candidate process/material pair rather than editing a stock template. No setting in this pack is an accepted PCTG or replacement-unit value.

Choose and calculate the candidate

Let B be your current candidate material Flow multiplier. A tile marked d means the intended relative percentage change for this comparison:

candidate material Flow multiplier = B × (1 + d / 100)

Pass 1 labels are −20, −15, −10, −5, 0, +5, +10, +15 and +20. These are the original coarse-test labels, not a requirement to print every extreme. Start near your baseline and use only justified changes. For example, if B = 0.98, the tile labelled +5 is tested at 0.98 × 1.05 = 1.029. This is arithmetic, not a recommended setting for your machine.

Pass 2 labels are 0 through −9. Rebase B to the selected coarse candidate; the same formula applies. Do not subtract the label directly from the multiplier or compound each new test on the previous tile. A finer intermediate comparison can reuse the zero-labelled mesh, with the actual value recorded externally.

Slice in Rocket

  1. In Prepare, select the intended printer template, ordinary Plastic material and Speedy mode. Identify the actual physical nozzle and material; a mode name or template label does not prove the output tool.
  2. Use Add models and open one STL from models/. Import in millimeters, flat on the plate, at 100% scale. Verify 40 × 30 × 2 mm.
  3. Use More Options → Plastics → Basic information → Flow multiplier on your copied material, then Apply. Copy the relevant OEM process group under Profiles, select its Speedy process, and select that custom group with the copied Plastic in Prepare. See matching selections. Enter the calculated value; do not change composite/fiber feed or a feature-only flow setting instead. Record the entered value and saved selection.
  4. Keep temperature, layer height, line width, walls, top/bottom thickness, infill, speeds and cooling identical between candidates. At a 2 mm specimen height, make sure the selected layer height and shell settings leave a broad, supported top with several solid layers. Do not assume Tinman's ten-layer scaling or shell overrides were imported. Do not stretch the model silently for a larger nozzle; document any deliberate geometry change as a new variant.
  5. Select Preview and let Rocket slice. Check every layer for a supported top, the intended ordinary-plastic paths, no fiber paths and a rate inside your established working region. Cooling and feature-speed limits can change timing.
  6. Check the selected material and actual output again before any send. Keep printer-specific starting, sensing and preparation behavior from the applicable OEM template. Do not run another slicer's G-code or paste firmware commands.

The exact material-editor click path and its emitted effect must be checked for your installed Rocket version. This working recipe does not claim that a hidden field or an imported label supplies the intended multiplier. Stop if you cannot identify the ordinary-plastic Flow multiplier or verify the output.

Read and confirm the result

After cooling, inspect the whole top under glancing light and with a light touch. Low flow leaves open channels and poor line joining. Excess flow creates raised ridges, nozzle plowing and piled-up line ends. Seek an even, closed surface without broad ridges; do not compensate for bad support below the top by increasing flow.

Record every tile's actual value, conditions and photographs. Confirm the chosen candidate in a fresh Rocket slice and a representative part. Cosmetic quality alone does not qualify strength or the complete material profile. See the guide's controlled tuning chapter.

Validation status

Mesh dimensions, source identity and triangle preservation are checked by the included tests for all 19 tiles. The zero-labelled sample was imported and sliced in Rocket 1.4.0.705 at 0.2 mm in ordinary-FFF Speedy mode. Separate native slices at material Flow multiplier 1.00 and 0.90 retained identical motion paths; their XY-deposition ratio was 0.89999921. E-only retraction recovery remained unchanged. This verifies that comparison, not every tile or all possible settings. Other tiles have not been individually sliced in this check.

Native observations and limitations are recorded in ROCKET_CHECKS.json. No tile has been physically validated by this pack. There is no production TUNED preset or ready-to-print job here.
