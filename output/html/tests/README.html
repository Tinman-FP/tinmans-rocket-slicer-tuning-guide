Rocket calibration test catalog

This catalog describes the question each test must answer. A mesh supplies geometry; it does not schedule temperatures, flow or pressure advance. Rocket must slice the test with your recorded tool, material, process and deliberate overrides.

Original Tinman flow geometry

The first flow pack reuses Tinman's existing coarse and fine flow tiles, with all source triangles preserved. It supplies 19 separate STLs, editable upstream originals, conversion source, licenses and a Rocket recipe. Tile labels are not imported modifiers: use one explicitly calculated material multiplier per separate Rocket slice.

All 19 meshes pass source/geometry checks. One representative zero-labelled tile has passed native Rocket import/slicing and a 1.00-versus-0.90 emitted-deposition comparison. No physical print, tuned preset, CFC qualification or native account project is included. Read the exact status.

Temperature and maximum-flow originals are linked below while creator-license verification and Rocket-specific test setup continue. The raw temperature asset is a 700 mm, wide-range source model; it must be correctly cropped, not compressed to make it fit. Numbers in its geometry do not set nozzle temperature.

The four earlier custom coupons are superseded local development history, not the agreed Tinman-geometry pack. They are excluded from publication.

Do not use a Tinman G-code file as Rocket output or assume an Orca 3MF preserves calibration modifiers when opened in Rocket. Do not distribute an account database, connected-printer project or private machine G-code as a generic test.

Temperature

Original asset: Tinman/Orca temperature tower, credited upstream to gaaZolee. Redistribution and a Rocket-ready crop remain pending; this edition links the source rather than redistributing it. Do not scale its Z height to select a range.

Use identical small coupons at separate fixed material temperatures unless a native height schedule is verified. Compare stable extrusion, supported details, bridges, stringing and cooled layer strength. Reject detached or motion-damaged specimens. Stay inside the actual material and hardware operating limits.

Maximum usable volumetric flow

Original asset: Tinman/Orca volumetric-speed test, credited upstream to CNC Kitchen. Creator-license verification and Rocket rate scheduling remain pending; no converted copy or automatic sweep is supplied here.

Use a long enough wall/path to reach the requested steady deposition rate. Compare fixed-setting runs and inspect actual flow in the emitted file; acceleration or cooling may prevent the requested speed from being reached. A cosmetic surface change, extrusion discontinuity and loss of layer strength are different limits. Record them separately and retain margin for the intended application.

Flow multiplier

Use a broad supported top surface with several top layers. Compare candidate material Flow multiplier values while holding temperature, speed, geometry and cooling fixed. Look for closed lines without raised ridges or nozzle plowing. Confirm the chosen value in a fresh job; do not tune global flow from the first layer alone.

Pressure response

Corner tests can reveal bulging or thinning, but no Rocket 1.4.0.705 pressure-advance field or automatic generator has been established. Keep OEM pressure behavior and record this stage as fixed/unqualified rather than inserting arbitrary firmware commands. A corner mesh alone is not an automatically varying PA test.

Cooling and retraction

Use supported bridge/overhang and separated-feature geometry after extrusion is stable. Change one cooling or travel/retraction variable. Keep matrix-only and fiber movement rules separate; never assume normal-plastic retraction is suitable for CFC.

Dimensions and fit

Measure fully cooled external spans, holes and fits at multiple locations. Separate proportional shrinkage from a constant contour/hole offset and bottom-layer flare. Do not alter axis calibration or global flow to hide a material shrinkage problem.

Composite and representative validation

Use Rocket's intended fiber/matrix mode, loading and paths. Inspect actual fiber placement, starts/ends, consolidation and interlayer behavior. Ordinary FFF coupons cannot qualify fiber length, cutting, matrix delivery or structural strength. Finish with a part that represents the actual application, and test relevant strength rather than declaring a structural profile from appearance alone.

Required release contents

Each public test needs model/source/license, an illustrated Rocket setup recipe, version/tool/nozzle/material scope, one-variable schedule, fixed conditions, output checks, interpretation, validation status and known limitations. A native Rocket project, when supplied, must reopen without private target/account state. Published results must identify tested conditions without implying universal safe values.
