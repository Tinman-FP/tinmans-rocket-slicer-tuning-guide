1. Establish the machine, material and job identity

Tuning means changing one setting, printing a controlled sample and deciding whether that change helped. It does not mean trying settings until a printer with a mechanical fault happens to finish a part.

This chapter comes before every material test. It applies separately to each physical FibreSeeker, not just to the model name shown in Rocket.

Know what this guide has and has not verified

The Rocket field survey for this edition inspected the installed macOS bundle labelled 1.4.0.705. Its other component version labels differ. Record the version that your application shows; do not treat an algorithm or exported-file version as an interchangeable application version. Field availability can change.

No native temperature, maximum-flow, flow-ratio or pressure-advance test generator was found in that inspected Rocket build. Its AI Calibration preprint option is not evidence of a filament pressure-advance calibration. The controlled tests in this guide therefore use ordinary models sliced in Rocket at fixed settings.

On October 19, 2026, the owner reported that the replacement FibreSeeker was received, online and still performing factory calibrations. Those are owner reports, not independently verified calibration passes or completed Rocket prints. Its formal physical-unit identity was still pending. Do not copy the retained machine's targets, offsets, tuning values, repair conclusions or experimental permissions to the replacement.

Understand the material paths

The manufacturer distinguishes three material paths, not three independent printing nozzles:

Path What goes through it What it does
Ordinary FFF plastic A supported plastic filament Prints ordinary plastic roads through the FFF tool
Composite plastic, also called matrix The plastic used with continuous reinforcement Provides the plastic around a reinforced route
Continuous fibre The specified continuous strand Reinforces a route through the composite system

Do not put continuous fibre through the ordinary plastic nozzle. Chopped-fibre filament, such as a plastic sold as CF-filled, is not the same thing as a continuous strand. A plastic nozzle's diameter does not prove that it is suitable for abrasive filled filament.

Old retained-machine exports used tool identifiers such as T0 and T1. Those files are useful historical evidence, not permission to guess the replacement's physical mapping. Match the selected tool to the actual nozzle, loading path and current OEM definition before printing.

Preserve the starting point

For a new machine, keep an as-found record before updates or unofficial changes:

  1. Assign a nonsecret shop identity that distinguishes this unit from every other printer of the same model. Keep serial numbers and connection secrets in private records, not in a published guide or shared screenshot.
  2. Record the firmware and Rocket versions, nozzle/tool labels, plate, supplied materials and visible condition. Photograph relevant assemblies and messages.
  3. Let active factory calibration finish. Save its actual results, including warnings or failed steps. An animation finishing is not proof that every calibration passed.
  4. Preserve available OEM profiles, projects and version information through supported export or backup methods. Do not install SSH merely to follow this guide. Record what could not be captured.
  5. If an OEM update is appropriate, record its source, version before and after, result and any requested recalibration. Never call an after-calibration backup a pristine pre-calibration backup.

This creates a factual record for troubleshooting and possible warranty review. It does not establish warranty coverage or a fault's cause. The old development unit's reported support or rights waiver is not a waiver for a new unit.

Inspect before changing material values

Check nozzle cleanliness and condition, filament-path resistance, spool freedom, fan operation, plate seating and the machine's supported homing/probing checks. Use the manufacturer's maintenance procedures. Do not change steps, rotation distance, tool offsets or hidden firmware limits to hide one spool's defect.

Stop if the nozzle leaks, the tool scrapes the plate, the feed grinds or stalls, the part moves, the machine loses position, or a fault appears. Photograph and record the event separately from material results. A leak is not an acceptable way to discover a material's maximum flow.

Prepare the actual plate using its manufacturer's cleaning and adhesive advice. Textured PEI, smooth coatings and other surfaces are not interchangeable. Keep plate preparation, bed temperature and the first-layer method consistent between comparison prints.

Verify the four descriptions of the same job

Description Beginner's check
Physical machine Correct unit, installed nozzle, material path, loaded spool and plate
Rocket printer selection Correct model, nozzle/tool definition and intended connection
Material and profile selections Correct plastic or matrix/fibre pair, temperatures and candidate revision
Preview and exported output Correct tool, dimensions, layers, material routes and intended settings

A familiar name is not enough. Recheck after importing a project, changing a mode, changing materials, selecting another printer or reopening Rocket.

For a new test, use a clean project and clearly identify its owning printer and job. Do not repurpose another operator's session or change its target while it is printing. Rocket work does not require opening TinManX1. Keep only the intended test on the plate and avoid old calibration projects that may retain overrides.

Before pressing Print, verify the connection belongs to this physical unit, review the preview, and keep the exact exported file with the test record. Use Rocket and the printer's supported preparation sequence; do not insert generic homing, extrusion or tool-change commands from another slicer.

First-layer pass rule

Watch the first layer. Adjacent roads should meet without deep ploughed ridges; the sample should stay attached through corners; the nozzle must not scrape. If the sample detaches, material observations after detachment are invalid.

Do not set global flow from the first layer alone. Plate texture, initial-layer height and nozzle-to-plate distance deliberately affect its appearance.

When the baseline repeats reliably, proceed to Controlled Tuning.
