Tinmans Rocket slicer tuning guide

A plain-language handbook for using Rocket Slicer to tune filament and understand the FibreSeeker 3. It adapts the experimental method in Tinmans Tuning Guide to Rocket's actual controls, templates, print modes, masks and fiber workflows.

Working edition 0.1, 19 October 2026. The installed reference is Rocket 1.4.0.705. Later-release additions are labeled separately. A model, profile or published test file is not evidence of a successful print on your machine.

Start with a known machine

Complete the manufacturer's machine calibration, preserve the current versions and settings, identify each physical tool/nozzle and confirm a simple Rocket print before tuning material. Do not change printer firmware, maintenance settings and filament settings together to solve one symptom.

Follow this route:

  1. Establish the baseline and tool identity.
  2. Run controlled material tests: conditioning, temperature, usable volumetric flow, pressure response where supported, flow multiplier, cooling/retraction, dimensions, seams and representative validation.
  3. Treat fiber and matrix as a separate workflow.
  4. Read the result before changing another setting.
  5. Keep records and release a reproducible profile.

When a copied material leaves the Profile selector empty, use the matching material and process instructions.

The sequence preserves the original guide's core. It does not imply that Rocket has the same automatic calibration generators, menu names, pressure-advance controls or project overrides as Tinman or OrcaSlicer.

Find a setting in plain language

The field reference follows the installed Rocket controls. Look up the label you see, then check its material/process/machine scope and print mode. Each entry distinguishes the control's purpose from a suggested experiment and identifies options whose exact behavior still needs confirmation.

Useful questions include:

  • Why is my part rough if the flow multiplier is correct?
  • Is Max volumetric speed the same as printing speed?
  • Does this material field affect normal plastic, composite matrix or fiber?
  • Is this option changing one model, one mask, one layer range or the whole job?
  • Why does a template or field disappear when I change print mode?
  • Will this setting repair a mechanical fault, or only hide its symptom?

Version and evidence rules explain how to use the reference when your Rocket version or hardware differs.

A stored parameter or translated label is not necessarily a selectable control. For example, a visible Max volumetric speed editor has not been established in this build. The tuning chapter uses verified feature speeds and exported-rate checks rather than an assumed MVS menu.

Calibration files

The test catalog pairs reusable geometry with Rocket-specific instructions and a validation status. Reusing a model does not carry over a temperature/speed/flow schedule. Slice the model in Rocket with your recorded machine, nozzle, material and process settings.

Download working edition 0.1.0-preview and the original flow tiles. The 19 meshes are source-checked; a representative zero tile has a native Rocket slice and multiplier-output comparison. Physical validation is still pending. Temperature/MVS conversion and additional native workflows remain in progress. Do not substitute Tinman-generated G-code for a Rocket slice. Keep the three kinds of setting separate

Machine settings describe the actual hardware and its calibration. Material settings describe a particular polymer or fiber/matrix combination on that hardware. Process settings describe how the part is built. A profile name is not proof that those settings match your loaded tool and spool.

Copy an OEM template before editing it. Keep the original as a control, name each candidate clearly, change one causal variable, save the complete project and final file, and confirm the selected value on a fresh representative part. Never use flow multiplier or dimensional compensation to disguise a loose, leaking or partially blocked hotend.

Safety and support records

Keep clear of hot surfaces and motion. Follow the manufacturer's loading, maintenance and material instructions. Watch an unproven test and stop on a leak, scrape, failed probe, lost adhesion, grinding or unstable temperature rather than trying to tune past it. Continuous fiber adds feed, cutting, path-length and layer placement conditions that an ordinary plastic coupon does not qualify.

Preserve original photos/logs, versions and settings before and after updates or modifications, including unsuccessful attempts. The experiment record is a practical starting point. A good record supports factual troubleshooting; it does not establish warranty coverage, certification or structural strength.

The public rebuild does not need an installed slicer, account database or private research directory.
